the mindset before the mechanics

read this before you read anything else

Trading is a probabilistic game. Every trade has a random outcome. Your edge is a statistical tendency — not a crystal ball. The market doesn't know you exist. It doesn't trigger your stop on purpose. It doesn't owe you anything. Success comes from accepting these truths deeply — not intellectually, but in your bones — and then executing your system with total discipline, trade after trade, regardless of what any single outcome tells you.

"Anything can happen. The only certainty is uncertainty." — Mark Douglas

pre-trade checklist

Seven yes/no questions before every trade. Pass: 7/7. Any "no" = skip. Non-negotiable.

  • Is my risk predefined in dollars?
  • Have I accepted the loss as possible?
  • Am I following my system — not FOMO?
  • Is my position size within rules?
  • Am I in a neutral emotional state?

key metrics to track

Win rate, expectancy, R-multiple, Sharpe ratio, max drawdown. Measure what matters — not P&L alone.

  • Expectancy = (win rate × avg win) − (loss rate × avg loss)
  • R-multiple: standardize every trade outcome
  • Max drawdown: the number that determines your sizing
glossary

key terms

probabilistic mindset
Accepting individual trade outcomes as random while trusting your edge produces positive results over a large sample.
edge
A statistical tendency for one outcome to occur more often than another over many trials. Does not predict any single trade.
expectancy
The average amount you can expect to win (or lose) per dollar risked per trade. Positive expectancy = your system works.
R-multiple
A standardized way to measure trade outcomes as multiples of your initial risk. +3R means you made 3× what you risked.
random distribution
Wins and losses from a given edge are scattered unpredictably. A 60% edge doesn't mean 6 of the next 10 will win.
market neutrality
The understanding that the market has no awareness of you. It operates without intent. Every tick is a neutral data point.
the zone
A mental state of total process focus, zero emotional attachment to outcomes. Execution becomes effortless. Fear evaporates.
max drawdown
The largest peak-to-trough decline in your account. If it exceeds your psychological tolerance, your sizing is too aggressive.
